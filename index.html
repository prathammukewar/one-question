    About

    One Question a Day works like Wordle, but for thinking. Every date has its own question, and everyone who visits on that date sees the same one. You write for a few minutes, keep what you wrote, and pass the question along if it’s a good one.

    There are 366 questions, one for each date including February 29. They come back every year on the same day, so October 1 always asks the same thing. When a question returns, you’ll see what you wrote the last time, but only after you’ve written this year’s answer.

    Streaks

    A day counts toward your streak when you answer that day’s question before your own midnight. You can still answer any past question from the archive. Those answers are marked late and don’t change your streak.

    Privacy

    Your answers are saved in this browser’s local storage and nowhere else. There’s no account and no server, so nobody else can read them. The flip side is that clearing your browser data deletes them, and answers written on your laptop won’t show up on your phone. Download a copy every so often.

    This copy of the site counts page views with GoatCounter. It records visits, never anything you type.

    Back up your answers

    Markdown is good for reading or posting. JSON can be imported into another browser, and importing merges with what’s already here.

    Daily reminder

    Pick a time and download a calendar file with a daily event that links back here. Open it with Apple Calendar or Outlook, or import it in Google Calendar’s settings.
